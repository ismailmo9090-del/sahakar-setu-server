# PMFBY Operational Details (फसल बीमा / Fasal Bima)

Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY / पीएमएफबीवाई) is the Government of India's crop-insurance / fasal bima scheme, implemented by the Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare and delivered through empanelled insurance companies (ICs) with support from banks, State Governments/UTs and the District / State administration. Coverage is **area based** for widespread yield losses and **individual farm based** for localized calamities and post-harvest losses. This document covers the operational mechanics: what is covered, how the Sum Insured and premium are fixed, who can enrol, the enrolment and claims process, claim documents, common rejection grounds, grievance routes and the principal changes brought in by the revised (PMFBY 2.0 / Kharif-2020) guidelines. Premium figures here are only a recap of the rates fixed by Government; refer to the scheme guidelines for the full legal text.

## 1. What PMFBY Covers

PMFBY insures notified crops grown in notified areas against a comprehensive set of non-preventable natural risks — from before sowing to the post-harvest stage. The State Government/UT notifies, for each season (Kharif and Rabi), the crops and the insurance units (typically districts, sub-districts or villages) to be covered, along with indemnity levels; only crops and areas appearing in that notification are insurable.

- **Standing crop / yield loss (basic cover).** Loss to the standing crop from sowing to harvesting due to non-preventable risks such as drought, dry spells, flood, inundation, wide-spread pest and disease attack, landslide, storm, hailstorm, cyclone and natural fire due to lightning. This is the core cover, assessed on an area-approach basis using crop yield data, and compensates the shortfall between the threshold yield and the actual yield of the season.

- **Prevented sowing / planting / germination.** When farmers who intended to sow a notified crop (and incurred preparatory expenses) are prevented from sowing, planting or achieving germination because of deficit rainfall or adverse seasonal/climatic conditions, a lump-sum payout of up to **25% of the Sum Insured** is paid. The provision is invoked by the State through a formal notification (typically where more than 75% of the crop-sown area of the notified unit remains unsown), and payment to affected insured farmers is made within 30 days of the notification. Once the prevented-sowing claim is paid, the insurance cover for that crop terminates for the season.

- **Mid-season adversity (on-account payment).** If adverse seasonal conditions — floods, prolonged dry spells, severe drought, etc. — occur mid-season and the expected yield of the affected crop is likely to fall below **50% of the normal/threshold yield**, the State may issue a damage notification and an "on-account" payment (up to 25% of the likely final claim) is released within about one month, later adjusted against the end-of-season yield-based claim. This provision is not invoked within 15 days of the normal harvest time.

- **Localized calamities.** Loss to notified crops from localized risks of **hailstorm, landslide, inundation, cloudburst and natural fire due to lightning** affecting isolated farms in the notified area. These are assessed on an individual-farm basis through a joint survey, and liability is limited to the proportionate Sum Insured linked to the cost of inputs incurred up to the occurrence of the peril. If the affected area under a notified crop exceeds 25% of the total insured area in the notified insurance unit, all eligible insured farmers of that unit are deemed to have suffered the loss and are compensated on a percentage-loss basis.

- **Post-harvest losses.** Coverage for crops kept in the field in "cut and spread" or small-bundled condition for drying after harvesting, for a maximum of **14 days (two weeks) from the date of harvesting**, against hailstorm, cyclone, cyclonic rains and unseasonal rains. Assessment is on an individual-farm basis through loss assessors and a joint survey with State officials.

- **Coverage option.** States/UTs may choose which add-on covers (prevented sowing, localized calamity, mid-season adversity, post-harvest) to implement and may even offer single-peril covers such as hail protection. Not all covers are available in every State/district — the State's own notification governs.

- **Exclusions.** Losses from war and kindred perils, nuclear risks, riots and malicious damage, theft, and destruction by domestic/wild animals are excluded, as are other preventable risks; post-harvest cover does not apply to crops already bundled/heaped for threshing.

## 2. Sum Insured: How It Is Fixed

The Sum Insured (SI) is the maximum liability of the insurance company for a farmer/crop in a season and the amount on which premium and claims are calculated. Two methods are recognised for fixing the SI per hectare, and the State/UT chooses which to apply for each district-crop combination:

- **Scale of Finance basis.** The District Level Technical Committee (DLTC), informed by the cost of cultivation, recommends a "Scale of Finance" (the loan eligibility per hectare) for each crop; the State/UTs adopt it. SI per hectare equals the Scale of Finance for the crop, and SI for a farmer = Scale of Finance × the notified/insured area sown under the crop.

- **Value of Notional Average Yield basis.** Alternatively, the SI may be fixed as the Notional Average Yield (NAY) of the crop in the district multiplied by the Minimum Support Price (MSP) of the current year; where no MSP is declared, the farm-gate price notified by the marketing department/board is used.

The actual yield of the season is estimated through Crop Cutting Experiments (CCEs) and, increasingly, technology-based methods such as YES-TECH, remote sensing and drones. For indemnity calculation the State also fixes an **indemnity level** (70%, 80% or 90%) per crop, and the **Threshold Yield (TY) = Average/Normal Yield × Indemnity level**. The yield-based claim formula is:

> Claim = (Threshold Yield − Actual Yield) ÷ Threshold Yield × Sum Insured

So a higher threshold yield or a larger Sum Insured increases the indemnity payable when yield falls. The premium is charged on the Sum Insured, and the actuarial/bidded premium rate applied to the SI determines the total premium for the season. Since the scheme's revision, the earlier practice of capping the Sum Insured (which used to shrink the insured value) has been removed, so farmers are indemnified against the full Sum Insured fixed for their notified crop and area. The State notification, tender documents and the National Crop Insurance Portal (NCIP) record the SI per hectare for every notified crop and district.

## 3. Who Is Eligible

All farmers growing notified crops in notified areas are eligible to insure — there is no landholding limit, and no distinction by category. Concretely:

- **Loanee farmers.** Farmers who have availed a short-term crop loan or Kisan Credit Card (KCC) for a notified crop from a bank/financial institution. Their enrolment is handled by the lending branch, and the premium is ordinarily debited to the loan account. With effect from Kharif 2020 the scheme is **voluntary for everyone, including loanees**; a loanee farmer who does not wish to be covered must submit a written declaration/opting-out form to the bank branch at least 7 days before the season's cut-off date, failing which the farmer is deemed covered. A farmer whose KCC/crop loan has turned **sub-standard** is not treated as a loanee for the scheme (the bank may still help such a farmer enrol as a non-loanee).

- **Non-loanee farmers.** All other farmers — small, marginal, large, and those who do not take crop loans — may enrol voluntarily through the prescribed channels (Common Service Centres, insurance company offices/agents, the crop insurance portal/app, and State-level facilitation centres, e.g. Rythu Bharosa Kendras in some States).

- **Sharecroppers and tenant farmers.** Expressly eligible. They must hold an insurable interest in the insured crop/land and submit State-recognised documentary proof — Records of Rights (RoR), Land Possession Certificate (LPC) or a valid tenancy contract/agreement/cultivation certificate as permitted by the State notification — because their name may not appear on the revenue record.

- **Documentary prerequisites (all farmers).** A valid Aadhaar number and a declaration of the crop sown or intended to be sown. Bank account details linked to Aadhaar enable direct benefit transfer (DBT) of claims.

- **Cut-off dates.** Every season a cut-off date is fixed and notified by the State/SLCCCI for enrolment and premium payment (typically around late June–July for Kharif and December–January for Rabi). Enrolment must be completed, and the premium debited/paid and received by the insurance company **on or before the cut-off date**; for localized calamity and post-harvest cover the premium must also have been received **before the occurrence of the peril**. Coverage is strictly limited to the SI per hectare notified for the crop multiplied by the farmer's insured area.

- **Priority segments.** Special effort is made to enrol SC/ST and women farmers, and Panchayati Raj Institutions are used for awareness and feedback.

## 4. Premium Rates and Government Subsidy Sharing

The farmer pays only a small, fixed share of the Sum Insured as premium; the rest of the actuarial (yield- and risk-based) premium is subsidised by Government.

- **Premium payable by the farmer** (cap on farmer's contribution, % of Sum Insured per season):
  - **2%** of the SI for all **Kharif** food and oilseed crops;
  - **1.5%** of the SI for all **Rabi** food and oilseed crops;
  - **5%** of the SI for **annual commercial and horticultural crops** (and the restructured weather-based scheme uses the same farmer rates for crops covered under it).

- **Government subsidy.** The balance between the farmer's 2%/1.5%/5% share and the actuarial/bidded premium is provided as a premium subsidy and shared **equally between the Central Government and the State/UT (50:50)**; for the **North-Eastern States the Central share is 90:10**. Subsidy is payable on the premium of every enrolled farmer, whether loanee or non-loanee, and there is no upper restriction on the Sum Insured on which it applies.

- **Precision on the Central cap.** From Kharif 2020 the Government of India's share of premium commitment for a notified crop is capped at **30% of the gross premium in unirrigated districts and 25% in irrigated districts** (a district with 50%+ irrigated area counts as irrigated). Any balance of the actuarial premium over the farmer + Central share falls on the State. In practice the subsidy each season is released by the Centre and the State directly to the insurance companies.

- **Remittance discipline.** For coverage to be valid the premium must be debited/paid and received by the insurance company **within the cut-off date** notified for the season (banks debit loanee accounts and remit farmer premiums with the farmer list, normally within 7 days of the cut-off). A farmer whose premium is not received in time — or is received only after a peril has already occurred — is not eligible for any claim. Non-payment or late payment of the State's subsidy share by the prescribed deadlines can disqualify that State from running the scheme in the next season, and delayed-release States additionally pay 12% interest.

- **Related costs.** Banks receive a service charge (about 4% of the farmer's premium) for loanee enrolment and remittance, and CSCs get agency commission for non-loanee enrolment.

## 5. How to Enrol

Enrolment is done season-wise before the notified cut-off date, through any of the channels below; the farmer must be quick because late enrolment is never accepted.

- **Loanee farmers (bank routed).** When a KCC or seasonal crop loan is sanctioned for a notified crop, the lending branch adds the premium to the loan amount, debits it from the farmer's account on the cut-off date and transmits the farmer's details to the insurance company through the National Crop Insurance Portal (NCIP). Loanee farmers need take no separate step unless they want to **opt out**, which they must do by written declaration at least **7 days before the cut-off date**. Farmers can also request a change of the insured crop name up to **2 days before the cut-off** for enrolment (earlier this required 30 days' notice).

- **Non-loanee farmers (direct channels).** Can enrol:
  - at any **Common Service Centre (CSC)** (the most common rural channel);
  - directly with the **empanelled insurance company** through its offices, field agents, or the company's portal/app;
  - online on the **National Crop Insurance Portal — pmfby.gov.in — or the Crop Insurance (PMFBY) mobile app**;
  - through State-run facilitation kiosks/Agriculture Department camps and, in some States, dedicated agricultural support centres.

- **Documents required for enrolment.**
  - Identity & eligibility: **Aadhaar** number, and **land record** — RoR/LPC/cultivation certificate or tenancy agreement (for sharecroppers/tenant farmers);
  - **Bank account details** (passbook/cancelled cheque) for claim DBT;
  - **crop and area declaration** — the crop to be insured, the season, and the sown (or intended-to-be-sown) area, consistent with the land record;
  - for loanees, the KCC/crop-loan documents (handled by the bank itself);
  - mobile number for claim/status notifications.

- **Premiums.** Non-loanee farmers pay the premium (2%/1.5%/5% of SI) at enrolment itself, online or at the CSC; loanee premiums are debited by the bank. The farmer receives an enrolment acknowledgment/insurance policy number, which should be kept safe.

- **Deadlines.** The State/SLCCCI fixes and notifies the cut-off date for each crop and season. Enrolment and (for non-loanees) premium payment must both be completed **on or before that date**; the insurance company must have received the premium by the cut-off for coverage to be valid.

## 6. Claims Process

PMFBY settles claims in two distinct tracks depending on the nature of the loss:

- **Widespread / yield-based claims (area approach — no individual intimation needed).** For drought, flood, dry spells, wide-spread pest/disease, etc., the farmer does not have to file any intimation or claim form. The State's agriculture/revenue machinery conducts the prescribed **Crop Cutting Experiments (CCEs)** — a minimum number per notified crop/insurance unit under the seasonality discipline — and uploads the actual yield data on the NCIP within the prescribed window (about two months of the notified CCE completion date). If the State does not furnish yield data in time, the claim is computed on technology-derived **synthetic yield** (yes, satellite-based / YES-TECH estimates). The portal auto-calculates the claim as: (Threshold Yield − Actual Yield) ÷ Threshold Yield × Sum Insured. The insurance company then validates and pays all eligible insured farmers. The District Level Joint Committee (DLJC) of State officials and the insurer finalises loss assessment for on-account and localized cases.

- **Localized calamities and post-harvest losses (individual-farm basis — intimation mandatory).** Because these are assessed farm-by-farm, the farmer must **intimate the loss within 72 hours** of the event (extended from 48 hours in the revised guidelines) — through the **toll-free helpline 14447**, the PMFBY Crop Insurance app, the insurance company, the bank/financial institution, or the State Government/district administration. The insurance company then appoints a **loss assessor within 48 hours**, and the physical assessment is a **joint survey** by the company's assessor, the block-level agriculture officer and the farmer. The loss is to be assessed and reported within about 10 days, and payment follows within the prescribed timelines (policy wording: roughly 15 days of the survey report; the Operational Guidelines: within 30 days of receipt of the survey report). If the affected area exceeds 25% of the total insured area in the notified insurance unit, localized/post-harvest loss is deemed for **all eligible insured farmers** of the unit based on the sample survey, instead of only individual applicants.

- **Prevented sowing.** The State declares the event by notification; the insurer pays the lump sum (up to 25% of SI) within **30 days of the State's order**, after which the cover for that crop ends for the season.

- **Mid-season adversity (on-account).** Where expected yield is below 50% of the threshold yield, the State issues the damage notification (within 7 days of the event) and the joint committee reports within 15 days; on-account payment (up to 25% of the likely claim) is released within about **one month**, later adjusted against the final yield-based claim.

- **End-of-season settlement timeline.** For the main yield-based track, claims are auto-calculated on the NCIP and insurance companies must **settle within 21 days of claim calculation on the portal**, subject to receipt of the premium subsidy from Government. Late payment carries an automatic **12%-per-annum penalty** (auto-levied through the NCIP since Kharif 2024); earlier guidelines prescribed settlement within two months of crop harvest/yield-data availability, also with a 12% interest penalty on delay. If a localized claim was lower than what the end-season area-approach claim turns out to be, the difference is paid at season end.

- All claim amounts are remitted by **Direct Benefit Transfer** to the farmer's Aadhaar-linked bank account.

## 7. Document Checklist for Claims

Farmers should keep the following in hand — the exact list is mostly relevant to localized-calimity, post-harvest and prevented-sowing claims, since widespread yield claims require no form:

1. **Intimation / claim form.** Duly filled and signed claim form with the crop, season, and area details; if information is not fully available at the time of loss, a semi-filled form may be lodged and the completed form submitted within 7 days of the loss.
2. **Land record / proof of cultivation.** RoR, Land Possession Certificate, or the tenancy agreement/cultivation certificate (for sharecroppers/tenant farmers) proving insurable interest.
3. **Sowing certificate / sowing declaration.** Sowing certificate or the crop/area declaration recorded on the portal (or the bank loan/sowing details for loanees), confirming the crop actually sown as insured.
4. **Premium receipt / policy evidence.** The enrolment acknowledgment, premium-payment receipt or policy number showing the premium was remitted before the cut-off date.
5. **Bank account details.** Aadhaar-seeded bank passbook/cancelled cheque details for claim DBT.
6. **Evidence of the calamity event.** Local newspaper cutting and any other available photographic evidence (preferably geo-tagged farm photos) substantiating the occurrence and severity of the hailstorm/landslide/flood/unseasonal-rain event.
7. **Survey / CCE report.** The joint survey report of the loss assessor and block agriculture officer (localized/post-harvest), or the CCE-based yield report / claim-calculation statement (widespread claims).
8. **Supporting loan documents (loanees).** KCC/crop-loan sanction details and the statement from the bank showing the premium debit.

Keeping copies and the claim registration number received on intimation (helpline 14447 or the app) also speeds up tracking and grievance filing.

## 8. Common Rejection Reasons

Claims are rejected or become non-admissible in the situations listed below — most fall to the farmer to avoid through timely enrolment and intimation:

- **Late intimation.** Missing the **72-hour** intimation window for localized calamities and post-harvest losses (individual-farm assessment cannot proceed without it). Widespread yield claims do not need farmer intimation.
- **Crop or area not notified / not insured.** The crop was not a notified crop for the season, or the land was outside the notified insurance unit, or the farmer never enrolled.
- **Premium not paid on time.** The premium was not debited/paid and received by the insurance company **before the cut-off date**; for localized/peril covers, the premium must also have been received **before the loss event**. Mere sanction/disbursement of a loan without premium receipt by the company does not make a farmer eligible.
- **Incorrect area / crop declared.** The declared sown crop or area does not match the land record or the seed/sowing declaration, or the farmer changed the crop without proper notice — the claim is computed only on the insured crop and area.
- **Incomplete or missing documents.** Claim not substantiated by land record, sowing certificate, premium receipt, photos or the completed claim form.
- **No insurable interest.** Claimant lacks a valid RoR/LPC/tenancy document; farmers with only non-agricultural collateral loans (e.g., gold or FD-backed loans) without insurable interest over the notified crop/land are not covered.
- **Sub-standard loan accounts.** Loanee farmers whose KCC/crop loan is classified sub-standard are not covered under the bank channel (they may enrol as non-loanee).
- **Excluded perils.** Loss arising from war and kindred perils, nuclear risks, riot/malicious damage, theft, animal grazing, or other preventable risks; post-harvest cover does not apply outside the 14-day cut-and-spread window.
- **Proxy indicators absent.** Prevented-sowing and mid-season adversity payouts require the State to have formally notified the event; absent such a notification, no payment under these covers.
- **Bank/beneficiary data errors.** Incorrect or unseeded bank account/Aadhaar details delay or block DBT of otherwise admissible claims.

## 9. Grievance Routes

A farmer who faces a rejected, delayed or disputed claim has multiple escalation routes in order of immediacy:

1. **Insurance company.** Every empanelled insurer maintains a **toll-free helpline, grievance cell and grievance officer**; the farmer should first lodge the grievance with the company's designated officer along with the claim/enrolment number. Companies are required by the operational guidelines to acknowledge and resolve complaints within defined timelines.
2. **National helpline 14447.** PMFBY's dedicated **toll-free number** for loss intimation, queries and complaints; additional help is available **300+ and the Crop Insurance App / NCIP portal** (pmfby.gov.in) where grievances can be registered and tracked online.
3. **Bank / Common Service Centre.** The bank branch (for loanees) or the CSC used for enrolment can register and escalate data-related complaints (premium, Aadhaar seeding, DBT details).
4. **District-level grievance mechanism.** The operational guidelines require a **grievance redressal machinery at sub-district, district, state and national levels**. In the first instance, the farmer should approach the **District Level Coordination/Monitoring Committee on Crop Insurance (DLCCCI/DLC)** headed by the District Collector, or the district agriculture officer, who can take up the case with the insurer operating in the district.
5. **State-level committees.** The **State Level Coordination Committee on Crop Insurance (SLCCCI)**, the State Government's agriculture department and the State Nodal Officer handle unresolved complaints and review insurance-company performance; the annual/seasonal district reviews and SLCCCI meetings are a genuine forum for individual cases to be raised by bankers and agriculture extension officials.
6. **Insurance Ombudsman.** If the insurer does not resolve a valid claim/dispute, the farmer may approach the **Insurance Ombudsman** (appointed under the Insurance Ombudsman Rules, with Regional Offices in Indian cities) for out-of-court resolution — this is the statutory route for insurance-company disputes, normally without cost, and its award is binding on the insurer.
7. **Union Ministry / CPGRAMS.** Grievances may be escalated to the **Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare** through the **Centralized Public Grievance Redress and Monitoring System (CPGRAMS)** and its PM-Kisan/crop-insurance helpdesk, and Members of Parliament/Assembly may be approached for larger systemic issues.

Where the delay is the insurer's fault, the automatic **12% p.a. penalty** on late claims is itself a lever — farmers can cite it when complaining about delayed settlement.

## 10. Revised PMFBY 2020 (PMFBY 2.0): Key Changes

The revised scheme, effective from **Kharif 2020**, restructured the scheme on "voluntary participation, technology-based assessment, and time-bound payment", with the following headline changes:

- **Voluntary for all farmers.** Insurance is no longer compulsory for loanee farmers; every farmer, loanee or not, effectively opts in, while a loanee farmer who wants to remain outside gives a written declaration to the bank **at least 7 days before the cut-off date** (otherwise the farmer is deemed covered). States were given flexibility to design coverage, choose between Scale-of-Finance and Notional-Average-Yield×MSP as the SI basis, and pick which add-on covers (prevented sowing, mid-season adversity, localized calamity, post-harvest) to implement, including single-peril covers such as hail.

- **Option to withdraw at district level / state exit.** States/UTs may **de-notify or withdraw the scheme for a specific district or notified unit/crop** within stated limits (deviation of up to 10% of notified units at the same premium before the cut-off, and complete withdrawal subject to terms), and non-complying States (e.g., materially late in releasing their premium-subsidy share) face **disqualification from implementing the scheme in the next season**. This supports states that were previously locked into full district coverage.

- **Technology for fast, honest settlement.** The revised guidelines lean on NCIP automation, satellite/remote-sensing based yield, **YES-TECH (Yield Estimation System Using Technology)**, **drones, Smart Sampling Technique** and reduced CCEs. Where State yield data is not uploaded within the prescribed period, claims settle on technology-derived **synthetic yield**. Claims are auto-calculated on the portal, cutting assessment disputes.

- **Time-bound settlement and penalties.** The guidelines prescribe **claim settlement within 21 days of claim calculation on the NCIP** (subject to receipt of premium subsidy) and payment of on-account/prevented-sowing/localized claims without waiting for the second instalment of subsidy; a **12% p.a. penalty** is auto-levied on insurers for late payment (from Kharif 2024). State delay in subsidy release also draws 12% interest and may bar next-season participation.

- **Better release of funds & monitoring.** States must budget the premium subsidy before the season; insurers get upfront subsidy (prompted release) enabling faster payout; a detailed SOP for **performance evaluation and de-empanelment of insurance companies**, and a transparent **grievance-redressal structure at sub-district/district/State/National levels**.

- **Widened but disciplined scope.** Perennial horticultural crops were brought within the ambit, and the cap on sum insured was removed; the GoI's premium-subsidy obligation is, however, capped at **30% of gross premium in unirrigated and 25% in irrigated districts** (50:50 sharing restored as 90:10 for North-Eastern States), and alternative risk-mitigation measures were provisioned for water-stressed districts.
