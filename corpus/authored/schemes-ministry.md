# Ministry of Cooperation — Schemes and Support for Cooperatives in India

The Ministry of Cooperation was created on 6 July 2021 by transferring cooperation-related subjects from the erstwhile Ministry of Agriculture, Cooperation and Farmers Welfare. It works on the vision of "Sahakar se Samriddhi" (prosperity through cooperation). The Ministry's principal agencies include the National Cooperative Development Corporation (NCDC), the National Council for Cooperative Training (NCCT), the National Cooperative Union of India (NCUI), the Central Registrar of Cooperative Societies, and, in the credit and development field, NABARD. Scheme finance reaches primary cooperative societies mainly through NCDC (term/investment loans and some grants) and through NABARD-linked credit lines, while education and training are delivered through NCCT institutes and State Cooperative Unions. This document summarises the principal Ministry-level schemes, the training ecosystem, and the routes by which cooperative societies access finance.

## Sahakar Mitra — NCDC Internship Scheme (2020)

Sahakar Mitra: Scheme on Internship Programme (SIP) is an internship initiative of the National Cooperative Development Corporation (NCDC). It was launched in June 2020, initially under the administrative oversight of the Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare and, since the creation of the Ministry of Cooperation in 2021, is associated with the cooperative development portfolio of that Ministry.

The objective of the scheme is to connect young people — school, university and college students and recent professional graduates — with the working of the cooperative sector, so that interns gain practical field experience while cooperative institutions access new and innovative ideas from young professionals. NCDC coordinates the placement of interns, provides the stipend from its earmarked funds, and monitors the internships through its regional offices.

Key features that have been officially published:

- Eligibility: professional graduates in agriculture and allied disciplines and IT, and students pursuing or having completed MBA degrees in areas such as Agri-business, Cooperation, Finance, International Trade, Forestry, Rural Development and Project Management. The scheme has also been expressed to give young professionals from academic institutions an avenue into leadership and entrepreneurial roles, including through Farmer Producer Organisations (FPOs).
- Stipend: degree/post-diploma-level interns receive a stipend of about ₹10,000 per month on a consolidated basis, with an additional lumpsum for report preparation under some published terms. Under the initial guidelines the total financial support over the internship period was ₹45,000 (four-month stipend plus report preparation amount).
- Duration: under the initial announcement, each intern received financial support over a four-month internship period. Duration and batch size are fixed for each admission cycle and have varied between cycles; confirm the exact duration and intake in the current notification on the NCDC portal (sip.ncdc.in or ncdc.in).
- Intake and placement: intake is limited (in the initial cycle, up to 60 interns were planned, with at most two interns placed at a time in a given NCDC regional office).
- Deliverables: interns work on cooperative-related project reports or business plans and submit a written report after the internship; applications are submitted through an online portal on the NCDC website.

The scheme helps youth gain work-ready skills, builds a pipeline of talent for the cooperative movement, and supports "Vocal for Local" and Atmanirbhar Bharat themes. It is distinct from (and complementary to) Yuva Sahakar, which provides project loans rather than internship stipends (see the Yuva Sahakar section). Internship terms are periodically revised, so exact amounts/periods should be confirmed against the current call for applications.

## Computerisation and Strengthening of PACS

The Ministry of Cooperation implements a Centrally Sponsored Project for the Computerisation of Primary Agricultural Credit Societies (PACS). The Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs approved the project in June 2022 for a period of five years (2022-23 to 2026-27, with sunset date 31 March 2027). The project originally targeted computerising about 63,000 functional PACS with a total financial outlay of ₹2,516 crore, of which the Government of India share was about ₹1,528 crore, State Governments about ₹736 crore, and NABARD about ₹252 crore. The outlay and the number of PACS covered have since been expanded as more States/UTs have come on board, and current coverage figures (sanctioned proposals exceeding 67,000 PACS from 30+ States/UTs, with a revised outlay of about ₹2,925 crore) should be checked in the Ministry's latest release.

What the project does:

- Brings all functional PACS onto an ERP-based common national software (called the National Level PACS Software), so that every PACS runs on a uniform, standardised platform.
- Links PACS with NABARD through State Cooperative Banks (StCBs) and District Central Cooperative Banks (DCCBs), integrating with the Common Banking Software (CBS) already in use by those higher tiers.
- Provides each PACS with hardware (computer, webcam, VPN, printer and biometric device), the ERP/common software, and a System Integrator to digitise legacy records, adapt and customise the ERP, and train staff.
- Implements a Common Accounting System (CAS) and Management Information System (MIS), now comprising a large set of ERP modules covering credit (loan origination, sanction, disbursement, recovery), deposits, accounting, audit, member management and procurement.
- Digitises legacy data including loan records, deposit records and membership records; the software supports Aadhaar-enabled services, integration with CBS of StCBs/DCCBs, PoS devices, Common Service Centres (CSC), the National Cooperative Database (NCD) and State-specific portals, and is multilingual.

Expected and realised benefits include: transparency and accountability in PACS operations; speedy disposal of loans and lower transaction costs for farmers; faster audit; reduced imbalances in payments and accounting with StCBs/DCCBs; digitisation of member, loan and deposit records; and enabling PACS to become multipurpose service points at the village/panchayat level (for example as Common Service Centres delivering 300+ e-services, and as Pradhan Mantri Kisan Samriddhi Kendras supplying agri-inputs). Around 13 crore farmers were expected to be covered by the original project, most of them small and marginal, and a large share of Kisan Credit Card lending flows through PACS. NABARD is the implementing agency; hardware procurement is done by States/UTs. Separate but parallel Ministry projects extend computerisation to the Registrar of Cooperative Societies (RCS) offices of States/UTs and to Agriculture and Rural Development Banks (ARDBs).

## Cooperative Education and Training — NCCT, RICMs/ICMs, VAMNICOM and National Cooperative Week

Cooperative education and training is delivered through a well-established institutional structure under the Ministry of Cooperation.

- NCCT (National Council for Cooperative Training) is an apex-level training council promoted and funded by the Ministry of Cooperation. It is the principal cooperative training body in the country and runs about 20 training institutions pan-India.
- The apex national institution is VAMNICOM — the Vaikunth Mehta National Institute of Cooperative Management, Pune — for senior-level cooperative personnel, also known for its two-year Post Graduate Diploma in Business Management / management programmes and Diploma in Cooperative Business Management for in-service personnel.
- Regional Institutes of Cooperative Management (RICMs), located regionally (for example Bengaluru, Gandhinagar, Chandigarh, Kalyani and Patna), serve groups of States; Institutes of Cooperative Management (ICMs) are located in State capitals (for example Bhopal, Chennai, Jaipur, Lucknow, Guwahati, Hyderabad and others). Along with these, Junior Training Centres (JTCs) provide foot-level training. Together these form the three-tier cooperative training structure (senior, middle and junior level).

Courses offered include:

- Higher Diploma in Cooperative Management (HDCM) — a longer programme (about 36 weeks/6 months in some centres; 6-9 months has also been published).
- Post Graduate Diploma in Cooperative Business Management — about 9 months.
- Cooperative Sector Diploma Programmes — about 12 weeks, focused on sectors such as dairy, handloom, cooperative audit, urban cooperative banks, housing cooperatives, fishery and computer applications.
- Certificate Course in Cooperative Management and short training/refresher courses, including accounting and computer application courses.

Target audience: managers and secretaries of PACS and other cooperative societies, cooperative employees, elected representatives, and officials of cooperative departments; NABARD-sponsored programmes for PACS managerial staff have also been conducted through NCCT units. The training aims to upskill cooperative personnel in management, accounting, governance and sector-specific business.

Member-level education (for ordinary members of cooperative societies) is primarily the domain of NCUI (National Cooperative Union of India) and the State Cooperative Unions, which organise member education, publicity and awareness activities. NCUI, the apex representative body of the cooperative movement (established 1929), also orientates college/university lecturers and coordinates publicity. Each year the All India/National Cooperative Week is observed around 14 to 20 November, commemorating the founding of the cooperative union movement; the Ministry and NCUI carry out public awareness and outreach programmes during this week. Courses, fees and durations are revised from time to time, and the exact fee and schedule for any course should be confirmed with the concerned institute or the NCCT.

## Yuva Sahakar — Cooperative Enterprise Support and Innovation Scheme (short reference)

Yuva Sahakar is NCDC's flagship loan scheme for young cooperators. Its full name is Yuva Sahakar — Cooperative Enterprise Support and Innovation Scheme; it was launched in FY 2019-20 and is implemented across the country by NCDC, a statutory corporation under the administrative control of the Ministry of Cooperation.

Under the scheme, NCDC extends project-based financial assistance to encourage newly formed cooperative societies with new and/or innovative business ideas. Cooperatives in operation for a minimum of 3 months that promote youth enterprise are eligible. NCDC created a dedicated fund for the scheme and linked it to its Cooperative Start-up and Innovation Fund. At launch, NCDC announced financing of up to 70% of project cost for general categories of youth cooperatives and up to 80% for cooperatives in special categories (North East region, Aspirational Districts, or cooperatives with women, SC, ST or PwD members), with interest at about 2% below NCDC's applicable rate for projects up to a certain cost and a moratorium on principal repayment in the initial period. The loan tenors and subvention features have been described as long-term (up to about 5 years) with a 2% interest subvention/incentive on the applicable rate; terms have been refined since launch, so exact funding limits and interest concessions should be confirmed from the current NCDC guidelines.

Loans under Yuva Sahakar can be dovetailed with subsidy available from other Government of India schemes. By late 2024, NCDC reported sanctioning financial assistance of about ₹47 crore to cooperative societies with nearly 19,000 beneficiary members under the scheme. For complete parameter details — eligible activities, margin money, ceilings and documentation — refer to NCDC's Yuva Sahakar operational guidelines or the scheme page on ncdc.in. This is a separate instrument from Sahakar Mitra (internship stipends) and from the general NCDC project-finance route described in the section on accessing scheme finance.

## Cooperatives Across Verticals — NCDC Financing; Farmers, Fisherfolk and Dairy Cooperatives

NCDC is the sole statutory institution devoted exclusively to financing the cooperative sector. Any cooperative society registered under a State Cooperative Societies Act or under the Multi-State Cooperative Societies Act is eligible for NCDC financial assistance for projects within the Corporation's purview, which spans agriculture and allied activities — including marketing, processing, storage, dairy, fisheries, livestock, handloom and sericulture, textiles, rural housing, sugar and food processing. NCDC provides term loans and investment loans, and in some cases working capital, and channels eligible subsidies by dovetailing with Central/State schemes.

Sectoral examples of the wide cooperative verticals served:

- Farmers' cooperatives: primary agricultural credit societies and marketing/processing cooperatives form the backbone; farmers' cooperatives also take up collective procurement, input supply and post-harvest infrastructure (see the storage/procurement section).
- Dairy cooperatives: India's dairy cooperative structure follows the three-tier "Anand pattern" — village dairy cooperative societies, district milk unions and State-level milk marketing federations (the best-known brand being Amul, marketed by the Gujarat Cooperative Milk Marketing Federation). The National Dairy Development Board (NDDB) is a statutory body (Anand; established 1965) that plans and organises dairy development and promotes cooperative milk unions; recent Ministry initiatives such as "White Revolution 2.0" aim to raise milk procurement by dairy cooperatives and expand their market reach. India has around 2 lakh primary dairy cooperative societies with over 1.5 crore members.
- Fisheries cooperatives: FISHCOPFED (National Federation of Fishers Cooperatives Ltd) is the apex national federation of Indian fisheries cooperatives. Ministry/Department initiatives promote fishery cooperatives and Fish Farmer Producer Organisations (FFPOs); NCDC supports fishery projects including modernisation, and fisheries cooperative societies have been strengthened by NCDC, for example for deep-sea trawling and processing.
- Ex-servicemen and other groups: cooperative training institutes conduct resettlement-oriented courses (e.g., certificate courses in computer applications and accounting management, and Higher Diploma in Cooperative Management courses) sponsored by the Directorate General Resettlement for ex-servicemen, enabling them to take up roles in the cooperative sector. NCDC also extends loans to cooperatives of SC/ST, women and other groups under inclusive initiatives.

The breadth of NCDC support has grown steadily; its annual disbursements to cooperatives have risen sharply in recent years. Exact figures, interest rates and ceilings for any vertical are project-specific and change with scheme guidelines, so confirm these with NCDC's latest circulars.

## Sahakar Kisan Sammunati and Cooperative Storage / Procurement-linked Initiatives (CPC, EWCS, multi-state PACS)

Several Ministry initiatives connect cooperative societies to the aggregation, storage and procurement of farmers' produce. Nomenclature and components of some of these farmer-facing schemes have evolved, so only general statements are made here and current notifications should be consulted for details.

- World's Largest Grain Storage Plan in the Cooperative Sector: approved by the Government in 2023, it envisages creating decentralised storage (godowns), custom hiring centres, primary processing units and associated agri-infrastructure at the PACS level by converging existing Government schemes such as the Agriculture Infrastructure Fund (AIF), the Agricultural Marketing Infrastructure (AMI) scheme, the Sub Mission on Agricultural Mechanization (SMAM) and the Pradhan Mantri Formalisation of Micro Food Processing Enterprises (PMFME). The plan reduces food-grain wastage and transport costs and helps farmers realise better prices. Under the pilot, godowns were constructed in selected PACS across States; FCI has been entrusted with identifying PACS godowns and providing hiring assurance for annual hiring.
- Procurement linkages: under crop-procurement initiatives such as the Pradhan Mantri Annadata Aay Sanrakshan Abhiyan (PM-AASHA), agencies like NAFED and NCCF procure crops such as pulses and oilseeds, and cooperative structures (including PACS and apex federations such as MARKFED) act as procurement and storage nodes. Centralised Procurement Cells (CPC) and electronic/negotiable warehouse receipt systems (EWRS/EWCS) are elements of this procurement-and-storage architecture that are being routed through cooperatives; the exact configuration and coverage of these cells and systems vary by State and by marketing season.
- Multi-state primary and other cooperatives: multi-state cooperative societies — including primary-level societies operating across more than one State — are registered under the Multi-State Cooperative Societies (MSCS) Act, 2002, as amended in 2023 to strengthen governance and transparency. The Ministry has also promoted establishment of new multipurpose PACS/dairy/fishery cooperatives to cover panchayats and villages not yet serviced by cooperatives, and has enabled PACS to diversify into multiple business activities under Model Bye-laws.
- Initiative names such as "Sahakar Kisan Sammunati" have appeared in Ministry communications for such farmer-facing cooperative procurement/storage work; because components, coverage and funding vary across seasons and States, always confirm the specific scheme, its current guidelines and financial terms with the Ministry of Cooperation or the concerned implementing agency (NCDC, NAFED, NCCF or the State government).

## How Cooperative Societies Obtain Scheme Finance

The general route for a cooperative society to access scheme finance is as follows:

- Eligibility and channel: the society must be validly registered under the State Cooperative Societies Act of its State or under the Multi-State Cooperative Societies Act, 2002 (as amended). Finance is typically obtained (a) directly from NCDC, (b) through the State Government/Apex Cooperative Institutions where NCDC or the State channels funds (for example, NCDC-to-State-to-society or NCDC-to-apex-federation-to-primary-society flows), or (c) through NABARD-linked credit via StCBs/DCCBs for credit-linked projects. Under some schemes, the Government of India share and the State share together fund projects, with the implementing agency (e.g., NABARD) coordinating, as in the PACS computerisation project.
- Preparation of project report: the society prepares a viable project report/business plan covering the activity, cost, working-capital requirement, promoter's contribution (margin money), projected returns and repayment capacity. For storage/infrastructure projects a detailed project report (DPR) is normally required.
- Submission and appraisal: proposals are submitted through the concerned Apex society/State Cooperative Department/Registrar of Cooperative Societies or directly to NCDC as per the specific scheme; the financing body appraises viability, security and repayment capacity.
- NCDC terms: NCDC lends for approved projects under its Concessional Financing at its applicable interest rates, sometimes with interest subvention/concessions (for example, the 2% concession and moratorium features of Yuva Sahakar), and up to a certain percentage of project cost depending on the category of cooperative; schemes can be dovetailed with subsidies from other Government schemes. Margin money (promoter's contribution) is generally required and its percentage varies by scheme and beneficiary category.
- State share: in many Centrally Sponsored projects, the State/UT government bears a prescribed share of the cost (for example, the PACS computerisation project splits the outlay between the Government of India, the States and NABARD). The exact interest rates, loan amounts, tenure, security margins and subsidy ceilings are scheme-specific and periodically revised; prospective cooperatives should obtain the latest guidelines from NCDC, the State Cooperation Department/Registrar, or NABARD.

## Producer Cooperatives and Farmers' Producer Organisations (POA-2020 context and FPO/cooperative route)

Institutions in which farmers own and run the enterprise collectively are referred to as farmers' producer organisations. Two main legal routes exist:

- Cooperative route: cooperative societies registered under State cooperative laws or the MSCS Act, 2002, including primary agricultural credit societies, multipurpose PACS, dairy and fishery cooperatives, and marketing/producer cooperatives. Farmer members hold shares, elect boards, and share surplus; this is the traditional cooperative model that the Ministry strengthens through computerisation, model bye-laws and new initiatives.
- FPO route: Farmer Producer Organisations (FPOs) — often promoted as farmer producer companies or societies — aggregate small and marginal farmers and provide services such as input supply, technical advice, collection and marketing. The Union Government supports formation of new FPOs, and NCDC has been allocated targets to form FPOs in the cooperative sector by strengthening PACS (for example, additional FPOs to be formed by PACS with NCDC support). Fish Farmer Producer Organisations (FFPOs) similarly aggregate fishermen.

The 2020 farm-sector reforms (the Farmers' Produce Trade and Commerce (Promotion and Facilitation) Act, 2020 and the Farmers (Empowerment and Protection) Agreement on Price Assurance and Farm Services Act, 2020 — sometimes referred to together as the farmers' produce/trade reform laws) were enacted to widen marketing options for farmers, including through electronic trading and contract-based price assurance. These 2020 enactments were subsequently repealed with effect from December 2021 and are no longer in force; therefore current agriculture marketing arrangements continue to operate through the existing APMC/Mandi framework and cooperative channels. The general purpose of producer organisations — whether cooperative or FPO route — remains to give farmer members collective bargaining strength, better access to credit, inputs, processing and markets, and a fair share of the final price. For promotion of FPOs/FFPOs, current scheme parameters (funding, clusters covered and support provided) are available from the Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare and the Department of Fisheries, with NCDC as the implementing agency for the cooperative-sector allocations.
