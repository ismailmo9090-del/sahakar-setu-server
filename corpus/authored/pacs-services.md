# PACS Services — Primary Agricultural Credit Society

This guide explains what a Primary Agricultural Credit Society (PACS) is and what services it offers to rural farmers and members in India. It covers the society's structure and membership, crop loans under the Kisan Credit Card (KCC), long-term loans, savings and deposits, sale of agricultural inputs, disbursement of government schemes, share capital and loan limits, loan recovery and default rules, and the digitisation ("smart PACS") programme under NABARD. Exact interest rates and charges set by individual societies vary by state and by-laws; where a Government of India scheme fixes a figure, that figure is stated.

## 1. What is a PACS — structure, membership and role

A PACS is the village-level grassroots unit of India's cooperative credit system. It is a cooperative society of farmers and rural residents registered under the State Cooperative Societies Act of the state in which it operates (multi-state societies register under the Multi-State Cooperative Societies Act, 2002, as amended in 2023). Each PACS has a defined area of operation — normally one village or a cluster of villages — and is administered by its own elected board under the supervision of the Registrar of Cooperative Societies (RCS) of the state. A PACS is not a bank, but it lends to farmers using funds it raises through member share capital, deposits and borrowing from the district and state cooperative banks.

The short-term cooperative credit structure (STCCS) is three-tier:

- Tier 1 — PACS at the village level, which lend directly to farmers.
- Tier 2 — District Central Cooperative Banks (DCCBs), which hold the dealings and lending limits of PACS, provide funds to them and supervise their day-to-day working.
- Tier 3 — State Cooperative Banks (SCBs, the "apex" banks), which sit at the head of the structure, link it to the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) and obtain refinance from NABARD.

Loans for the same chain flow downward: NABARD refinances the SCBs, the SCBs finance the DCCBs, and the DCCBs finance the PACS. There is a parallel long-term structure of Primary and State Cooperative Agriculture and Rural Development Banks (PCARDBs/SCARDBs) for investment credit. NABARD supervises SCBs, DCCBs and the long-term cooperative banks.

Membership is open and voluntary. Eligibility and rules generally include:

- Adults residing or farming in the society's area of operation; the core members are farmers, including small, marginal, tenant and sharecropper households.
- A small admission/membership fee plus purchase of the minimum number of shares required by the by-laws.
- One member = one vote in the general body, regardless of how many shares the member holds.
- The general body elects the board of directors from among members; state acts and by-laws commonly reserve seats for women and for Scheduled Castes/Scheduled Tribes and weaker sections.
- Members are expected to borrow through the society in whose area they operate, and to repay loans taken under the society's credit discipline.

In the cooperative credit system the PACS is the "face" of credit at the village: it assesses a farmer's genuine credit need, disburses short and medium-term loans, recovers repayment after harvest, and enforces repayment discipline. Beyond lending, a PACS performs many non-credit functions — retailing seeds, fertilisers and pesticides, running godowns and sometimes fair price shops, acting as a banking correspondent for the DCCB, facilitating PMFBY crop insurance enrolment and KCC issuance, and channeling several government schemes to members.

*हिंदी: PACS = प्राथमिक कृषि साख सहकारी समिति — ग्राम स्तर पर काम करने वाली सहकारी समिति, जो किसानों को फसल व अन्य कृषि ऋण देती है और सरकारी योजनाओं की सुविधा देती है।*

## 2. Short-term crop loans (KCC-linked)

Short-term crop loans are working-capital loans for one cropping season. Their purpose is to meet the cash and kind expenses of cultivation — seeds, fertilisers, pesticides, hired labour, irrigation charges, machinery hire and the family's basic needs during the season — so that the farmer can grow the crop without selling assets or borrowing from informal money-lenders.

The main instrument is the Kisan Credit Card (KCC), a revolving credit facility introduced in 1998 and operated through the cooperative channel by PACS and cooperative banks. Salient features:

- The credit limit is fixed from the *scale of finance* — the district-level notified per-hectare cost of cultivation for each crop — multiplied by the area the farmer will sow, plus 10% of that amount as contingency for price/cost changes.
- The KCC limit also covers post-harvest storage and the household's consumption needs; the consumption component is capped at about 10% of the credit limit.
- The card is valid for a fixed term (commonly five years) and is reviewed/renewed yearly; it is revolving, so once the member repays, the money can be drawn again without a fresh application.
- Because scale of finance depends on the crop, soil, irrigation and the district, the loan amount varies by farmer and area.

Interest: banks and the cooperative channel charge a concessional rate on crop loans up to ₹3 lakh. Under the Government of India Interest Subvention Scheme (revamped in 2022), farmers receive 1.5% interest subvention on the loan, and those who repay on time get an additional 3% prompt-repayment incentive, so the effective rate for timely repayment is about 5% per annum. The subvention is routed through NABARD and the cooperative banks — a Government of India figure; any additional state subsidy varies by state.

Security: crop loans below prescribed limits generally require no tangible collateral — no mortgage of land and no property security. The primary security is a *crop lien*: the society takes a hypothecation/charge over the standing crop, and repayment is expected to come from the sale proceeds of that crop after harvest. For limits above the prescribed ceiling or for higher-risk borrowers, the by-laws or the financing bank may ask for a third-party guarantee or additional security.

Repayment is timed to the harvest cycle. Repayment collections are made at the society counter when the crop is sold (often through a mandi, godown or the society's marketing tie-up). If the crop fails due to drought, flood, hailstorm or pest, the member should report immediately to the society; loans can be rescheduled or restructured, and under recognised norms a failed short-term crop loan may be converted into a medium-term loan. Timely repayment also keeps the KCC revolving and preserves the borrower's eligibility for the prompt-repayment incentive.

*हिंदी: फसल ऋण (crop loan), किसान क्रेडिट कार्ड (KCC), ब्याज दर (interest rate), समय पर चुकाने पर ब्याज छूट — फसल ऋण किसानों को बुवाई से लेकर कटाई तक की खर्चे पूरे करने के लिए मिलता है।*

## 3. Long-term loans

Long-term (investment) loans at a PACS are given for durable assets and land improvement whose benefit lasts several years. The idea is that the asset itself increases the farm's productivity, and the increased income repays the loan in instalments. Common purposes:

- Land development — land levelling, bunding, drainage, soil conservation and reclamation of waste land.
- Irrigation — digging of wells and borewells, installation of pumps and motors, and micro-irrigation such as drip and sprinkler systems.
- Farm machinery — tractors, power tillers, threshers, seed drills and other implements.
- Post-harvest assets — farm godowns, silos, drying yards.
- Allied and rural activities — orchards and plantation, livestock purchase and sheds, fisheries and poultry, depending on the by-laws.

Tenure: the term matches the expected life of the asset and the stream of income it produces. Medium-term loans are commonly 3–5 years; long-term loans can run up to 10–15 years. The period varies with the purpose, the member's repayment capacity and state/refinance norms, so no single tenure applies. Repayment is by instalments — monthly, half-yearly or harvest-linked — and the principal is reduced over the loan period, with interest on the reducing balance.

Funding and channel: traditionally, long-term investment credit was the business of the long-term cooperative structure (PCARDBs/SCARDBs). In practice PACS also give medium/long-term finance or forward the member's requirement to the DCCB and the financing bank, because the funds a PACS lend come from the DCCB/SCB chain and are refinanced by NABARD. The KCC has also been widened to cover term loans for farm machinery and equipment, so a member may get durable credit through the same card. The exact product (society loan, DCCB loan or refinanced term loan) varies by state.

Documentation: because these are bigger, longer loans, the society needs more evidence than for a crop loan. A member applying for a term loan is normally asked for:

- A written application stating purpose and amount; the society's loan application form.
- Proof of identity and membership (Aadhaar, photograph, membership/share certificate).
- Title/possession evidence for land — land records, patadar passbook/patta, khasra details.
- For land development or irrigation — a cost estimate, sometimes a technical report for wells/borings.
- For machinery — quotations or invoices for the tractor/pump/implement to be purchased.
- Repayment plan and, where required, guarantors.

Security mirrors the purpose: a mortgage of land/property for big loans and hypothecation of the purchased asset (for example the machinery or pump) for others, with guarantors when the board requires them. Loan appraisal considers repayment capacity, security available, end-use and the member's past record, and the loan is disbursed against bills/estimates so that it is actually spent on the declared asset.

*हिंदी: दीर्घकालीन ऋण (long-term loan) — भूमि विकास, सिंचाई और कृषि मशीनरी के लिए; किस्तों में (in instalments) चुकाया जाता है; दस्तावेज़ में भूमि रिकॉर्ड और अनुमान/कोटेशन शामिल होते हैं।*

## 4. Savings / fixed deposits at PACS

Apart from lending, a PACS mobilises savings from its members, and this deposit base, together with share capital and borrowings, funds the society's lending. Most societies offer:

- A savings (passbook) account, where members can deposit and withdraw small amounts at any time.
- Term/ fixed deposits (FD) and recurring deposits, where money is locked for a fixed period and earns a higher rate.
- In many states the society also accepts deposits from non-members, but the main product is the member savings account.

Features of PACS deposits:

- Interest rates are not fixed by any single central rule — each society's board fixes its rates in the by-laws, within state cooperative-deposit norms. Rates therefore *vary by society*, and a PACS may pay more or less than a commercial bank. The rate on fixed deposits is usually higher than the savings rate.
- There is no universal deposit insurance for society deposits: deposit insurance under the Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation (DICGC) covers commercial and cooperative *banks*, but PACS deposits are generally not covered by DICGC. Some states have their own guarantee or proposal under discussion, so a member should check the position in their state and by-laws.
- Withdrawals from savings are allowed as per the society's rules (usually a withdrawal slip signed by the account holder), subject to the account holder's KYC documents. Fixed deposits can generally be withdrawn before maturity only on the society's penal terms, which also vary by society.
- Nomination: a member can register a nominee for a deposit. On the death of the depositor the society pays the deposit to the registered nominee (nomination normally prevails over unregistered claims), subject to the society's verification of documents.
- Account discipline: the society credits interest periodically, issues a passbook and statement, and now records transactions in its computerised system or common accounting software where digitised; members should check entries for correctness.

Deposits with a PACS are part of the society's owned resources, so a member who both borrows and saves helps the society's viability; some societies also use the deposit balance as a set-off against the member's dues when due, in accordance with the by-laws.

*हिंदी: बचत खाता (savings account), सावधि/फिक्स्ड जमा (fixed deposit), ब्याज दर समिति के नियमों के अनुसार अलग-अलग होती है, नामांकन (nomination) से मृत्यु के बाद राशि वारिस को मिलती है।*

## 5. Agricultural inputs — khaad, beej, pesticides

Many PACS double as the village agro-input shop. Through a society store, godown or a tie-up with the district/state cooperative marketing federation, a PACS supplies:

- Fertiliser (khaad) — urea, DAP, NPK and other complex fertilisers and micronutrients.
- Seed (beej) — certified/truthfully-labelled seeds of field crops and horticultural crops, and at times seed meant for public/seed-distribution schemes.
- Pesticides and weedicides, and occasionally bio-inputs, vermicompost and plant-protection items.

Key facts for members:

- The society procures inputs from authorised sources (the DCCB, state marketing federation or registered companies) and sells at the notified/ maximum retail price (MRP). In the case of fertiliser, the Government of India bears the subsidy through the manufacturers under the urea policy and the Nutrient-Based Subsidy (NBS) regime, so the farmer pays the subsidised MRP at the counter; the PACS itself is the selling dealer. The same general principle of notified/scheme price applies to subsidised seed in states that notify it.
- Availability is seasonal and stock is finite. Members are expected to *book* their requirement before the sowing season — with the secretary or at the store — so that the society can procure the right quantity; booking also protects the member against mid-season shortage.
- Credit linkage to the crop loan: the crop loan limit includes a kind (input) component. When a farmer draws the crop loan, the society can supply the inputs against that sanctioned limit and adjust the input cost against the loan amount, or the member buys cash at the store and the purchase is recorded. This is the standard "loan includes inputs" arrangement, so repayments and input records stay in one account.
- Quality control: certified seed and dated pesticide batches are preferred; the member should check the pack, label and expiry and retain the bill. A complaint over defective input — spurious seed, adulterated fertiliser, expired pesticide — can be taken to the society, the office of the Registrar of Cooperative Societies, or the district agriculture department, and the society records the complaint.
- Some states route seed and input subsidy, calamity input relief, or special season campaigns through the cooperative channel, so the PACS store is often the first place a farmer hears about schemes and can apply under them.

In short, the input store is not a separate business: it ties credit to inputs, improves timely availability, and keeps a single record of what the farmer received and owes.

*हिंदी: खाद (fertiliser), बीज (seed), कीटनाशक (pesticide) — ये समिति के गोदाम/दुकान से मिलते हैं; मौसम से पहले बुकिंग करनी चाहिए, और कीमत फसल ऋण से जोड़ी जा सकती है।*

## 6. Government scheme disbursement through PACS

The PACS is the last-mile agency through which several government schemes reach the farmer. Its exact role differs by scheme:

- Fertiliser subsidy: the PACS sells fertiliser at the subsidised MRP; the subsidy itself is paid by the Centre to the manufacturers, not handed over in cash at the society. The member's benefit is the lower counter price.
- PMFBY crop insurance: the PACS and the cooperative channel are authorised points of enrolment/facilitation under the Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana. The farmer's small premium contribution on an insured crop loan is typically auto-debited from the KCC credit limit at the time of loan drawal, the society furnishes notified-area/land details, and on declaration of loss the claim is credited to the farmer's insured account. The PACS helps the member with notices, documents and claim follow-up.
- Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT) and state schemes: in many states input subsidy, seed subsidy, and relief after natural calamities are routed through the cooperative network. The PACS verifies eligibility, prepares/enrols beneficiary lists, disburses amounts (by DBT into the member's bank account or as society payments) and maintains the records.
- KCC and interest subvention: as the issuing/operating society for KCC crop loans, the PACS prepares the claims through which the member gets the subvention and the prompt-repayment incentive described in Part 2.
- Other cooperative/farm schemes: wages and purchase rates fixed for the area, market support work, and state-specific grant/lending programmes may also flow through the society, depending on state policy.

Eligibility is set by each scheme, not by the society. Typical conditions involve the member being a land-owning or tenant farmer, cultivating a notified area/crop (for PMFBY), being enrolled/eligible under the state's beneficiary list (for subsidy), and maintaining a good repayment record for credit-linked benefits. The PACS cannot waive scheme conditions; it implements them.

Status checks: for PMFBY, the claim and premium status appear on the PMFBY portal/web at the policy/claim stage; for DBT amounts, the status appears under PFMS and the farmer's own bank passbook; for cooperative/state subsidies, status can be checked with the PACS secretary or on the state cooperative/portal. Members should ask the society for the loan/account statement and keep the relevant scheme ID (KCC number, policy number, application ID) to track payments.

*हिंदी: सरकारी योजनाओं का पैसा/लाभ PACS के माध्यम से मिलता है — खाद सब्सिडी दुकान की कीमत में, फसल बीमा (PMFBY) KCC से कटकर, और अन्य अनुदान सीधे खाते में।*

## 7. Share capital, membership fees and loan limit

Becoming a member: a person applies to the society in the prescribed form, pays a small admission/membership fee, and buys the minimum number of shares fixed by the by-laws. Share values are set in the by-laws and *vary by society* — a common pattern is a modest per-share value with a requirement of one to a few shares — and a member may buy more shares to raise the qualifying share capital. Whatever the shares, one member has one vote.

Share capital is the society's own (owned) fund. It is not a deposit: it stays as the member's capital stake, earns a dividend only if the society makes a profit and declares it (subject to statutory reserves and the by-laws), and is refundable on leaving the society as per the by-laws, normally after settling all dues. The society's share capital is one of the bases on which the DCCB sanctions the society's overall borrowing limit.

Loan limit: the board fixes each member's borrowing limit. It is not a fixed all-India figure; it is computed per member from:

- Repayment capacity — net farm and family income after essential family expenses.
- Landholding and the area actually cropped, applied against the notified scale of finance for the crops grown.
- The member's share capital/owned contribution and the security available.
- Past repayment record — a clean repayer gets a higher limit; a defaulter is reduced.
- For KCC loans, the formula is scale of finance × cropped area, plus 10% contingency, plus the household consumption component (up to about 10% of the credit limit).

The society operates within an overall lending/credit limit that the DCCB sanctions against the society's owned funds, deposits and security. Within that ceiling, the board's resolutions fix and periodically review individual limits. New members usually complete a short qualifying period and prove their capacity before a full limit is sanctioned. Members should maintain a single borrowing relationship with the society in whose area they operate, keep their loan account clean, and review the limit each renewal/season; the secretary can explain how the limit is computed in that society.

Because the limit depends on land, crop, capacity and history, there is no fixed "maximum" for every member — the important thing is that the limit is a need-and-capacity-based figure fixed transparently by the board.

*हिंदी: शेयर पूँजी और सदस्यता शुल्क (share capital & membership fee) से सदस्य बनते हैं; ऋण सीमा (loan limit) शेयर, ज़मीन, फसल क्षेत्र और चुकौती इतिहास के आधार पर तय होती है।*

## 8. Loan recovery and what happens on default

Normal recovery: PACS loans are repaid from farm income, principally after harvest. The member pays at the society counter; for a crop loan the society's lien on the crop is the first claim on sale proceeds. The society tracks due dates, sends reminders, and a member who repays on time keeps the KCC revolving and stays eligible for incentives.

What counts as default: an instalment or repayment amount left unpaid after its due date. On default the society:

- Adds overdue interest/penalty as per the by-laws and policy — the overdue rate and grace period vary by society.
- Stops or restricts fresh lending to the defaulter until the account is regularised.
- Reports the account as non-performing/overdue under the applicable state and NABARD norms, which affects the member's future borrowing.

Recovery process on default (in order):

1. Notice and demand — a written notice/statement to the borrower, and to the guarantor/surety if there is one, asking for payment within a fixed period.
2. Personal contact — the secretary or the recovery staff/officer discusses the account, offers instalment options, and tries to resolve the matter voluntarily before harsher steps.
3. Legal steps under the Cooperative Societies Act — where a debt remains unpaid, the society may refer the matter to arbitration/the Registrar and obtain an award or recovery certificate. The certificate can then be executed by recovery machinery: attachment and auction of the secured/mortgaged property after due process, or set-off against the member's deposits, as the by-laws and law allow.
4. Guarantors — the surety becomes liable to the extent of the guarantee.

No-harassment rules: recovery must be lawful and orderly. Neither cooperative law nor RBI/cooperative fair-practice norms permit recovery through humiliation, force, wrongful detention, or seizure of the bare essentials of a rural household or productive stock beyond the secured property. A borrower facing harsh or unlawful recovery can complain to the society board, the DCCB, the Registrar of Cooperative Societies, or the district cooperative office, and can ask for a resettlement/instalment plan.

Genuine crop failure is a recognised defence: on drought, flood or pest loss the member should immediately inform the society, because loans can be rescheduled or restructured, and a failed short-term loan may be converted to a medium-term loan; state-specific debt-waiver or one-time settlement schemes, where in force, also offer relief. The honest default is treated differently from the wilful defaulter; transparency and early communication protect the member.

*हिंदी: समय पर वसूली के लिए नोटिस और कानूनी कार्रवाई होती है; उत्पीड़न/परेशान करना ग़लत है — फसल खराब होने पर तुरंत समिति को बताएँ ताकि ऋण पुनर्गठित हो सके।*

## 9. Cyber / smart PACS digitisation under NABARD

To modernise the cooperative network, the Government of India launched a national programme for the computerisation of PACS, implemented with the Ministry of Cooperation, NABARD, state governments and the RCS machinery. The programme (central assistance of over ₹1,500 crore, targeting approximately 63,000–67,000 PACS) aims to put every PACS on a common, connected digital platform. General, stable facts:

- Each PACS gets hardware (computer/laptop and printer), an internet connection, and a Common Accounting Software (CAS/ERP-style system) so that the loan book, deposits, share capital and daily transactions are recorded electronically and the day's data flows to the DCCB online.
- Members are enrolled with e-KYC (Aadhaar-linked identity) so that identity, shareholding and accounts are verified digitally, and each society gets a unique digital identifier used across the DCCB and NABARD portals.
- Digitisation improves transparency of accounts, makes audits easier, speeds up KCC and interest-subvention claim processing, keeps PMFBY premium data in order, supports DBT facilitation, and reduces paperwork/routing through many registers.
- Multipurpose/member smart cards: in the digitised model, members can be issued multipurpose cards that record identity, membership and account data, and that link with the KCC and the society's digital records. Where issued, a Rupay-linked KCC or a society member card lets the member withdraw pension/DBT and transact at the society or the associated bank point without fresh paperwork.
- Banking correspondent linkage: NABARD supports PACS to work as Business Correspondents/agents of DCCBs and scheduled banks. Through this, the PACS staff operate a small banking window — deposits, withdrawals, and DBT/benefit payouts — inside the village using handheld and Aadhaar-enabled devices, so that unbanked rural households get banking services at their own cooperative. This is the "banking-window / banking correspondent" model promoted for smart PACS.
- Cyber safety: with digitisation come data-security duties. The society must keep backups and protect member data; members must not share passwords, OTPs or PINs, and should report suspected fraud to the society, the associated bank, and the local cyber cell. NABARD and state agencies run training and digital-literacy programmes for PACS secretaries and computer operators, together with financial-literacy camps for members.

Because the programme is being rolled out state by state, a specific society may still be paper-based or partially digitised. Members can ask the secretary whether their PACS is computerised, whether cards/BC services are live, and how to update their KYC — participation is voluntary but beneficial, and the digitised society gives a passbook/statement in print or digital form.

*हिंदी: स्मार्ट PACS (smart PACS) — कंप्यूटरीकरण से खाते पारदर्शी होते हैं, सदस्यों को मल्टीपर्पज़ कार्ड/बैंकिंग कोरेस्पोंडेंट सुविधा मिलती है; OTP/पासवर्ड किसी से साझा न करें।*
